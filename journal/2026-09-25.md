## Discussion

Brought Mikey up to speed on the combined topic idea from the last meeting, then read the two papers Mark found for his action from last week.

**Paper 1:** Raileanu, L. & Stoffel, K. (2004). _Theoretical Comparison between the Gini Index and Information Gain_ [Paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226475848_Theoretical_Comparison_between_the_Gini_Index_and_Information_Gain_Criteria)

- They characterise mathematically when the 2 pick the same split, so you can tell whether Gini and Information Gain will agree on a dataset **without building the trees**.
- **The finding that worries us:** disagreement "was never higher than 2% of all cases". If the criteria agree ~98% of the time they build much the same tree, and our research question likely answers "no difference" — the null hypothesis in Skye's proposal.

**Paper 2:** Wang, Y., Song, C. & Xia, S. (2016). _Unifying the Split Criteria of Decision Trees Using Tsallis Entropy_ [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.08136)

- Shannon entropy, Gain Ratio and Gini index all fall out of Tsallis entropy with a tunable `q`: Gini is exactly `q = 2`, Shannon the `q → 1` limit.
- They score trees on accuracy **and total node count**. On Glass, accuracy peaks at `q = 2.6` but node count is lowest at `q = 3.9`
- Neither paper measures interpretability — nobody asks whether a human can read the tree or whether the top splits are stable across reruns. That's where our project sits.
